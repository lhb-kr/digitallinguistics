02 / APPROACH

Two axes, not intuition

A streaming library cannot be sorted by impression. We measured each play along two independent, data-backed dimensions and let the combination define the type.

Axis I

Emotional Profile

How sentiment moves through the play: its direction, its turbulence, and how often it crosses its own centre. Five arc families emerge — Pendulum, Ascendant, Collapse, Steady, Outlier.

Axis II

Relational Profile

How characters are connected and distributed: who is central, how dense the web is, and whether the world is one connected field or a set of bounded enclaves. This collapses to two Social Fields — Networked and Bounded.

Final Type = Sentiment Type × Social Field. The emotional axis says how the feeling moves; the relational axis says the space it moves through. The two sections that follow show how each axis was built — including the dead ends we worked through.

02
Second error — the wrong model & window

VADER, smoothed too loosely

VADER — a rule-based, modern-English lexicon — misread Early Modern English and returned inaccurate scores. We switched to a deep-learning classifier (siebert) and tightened LOWESS smoothing from a 200-unit window to 150, which kept the broad arc while recovering detail the looser window flattened away.

Play: Window: tighter (more detail)

Drag to widen the smoothing window on a real arc: a looser window (right) erases small reversals; the tighter window we adopted keeps them. ▬ looser · ▬ tighter.

Solution: DL (siebert) + LOWESS 150. All arcs in this report use those settings.

03
Third error — the zero-baseline blind spot

Why a flat zero hides the story

Reading sentiment against a fixed zero line is deficient: a play can sit far from zero yet still swing meaningfully, and a play hugging zero can still reverse. We rebuilt the tool on top of the professor's original site, adding three reference lines — start–end midline, overall mean, and first/last-scene average — and counting crossings against each.

04 / RELATIONAL PROFILE

The Character Network

Why these tools, what data, and the four structural questions we asked of every play — before collapsing them into one relational axis.

Tools & why

DraCor supplies a clean, structured co-occurrence corpus for all 37 plays, so relationships are extracted consistently rather than by hand. Gephi (and Gephi Lite) then computes the structural metrics — degree, density, average path length, modularity, edge weight — and lays the graph out for inspection. We built a custom viewer to host the exported graphs.

What we read

Node colour = modularity class (community); node size = degree (with word-count as a secondary cue); edge thickness = weight (how often two characters share a scene). Layout used ForceAtlas2 + NoOverlap — but final judgement always deferred to the numbers and the edge distribution, not visual position. A thick edge means frequent co-appearance, not affection.

The subsumption step

Four fine-grained criteria would shatter the library into too many types, so we rolled them into broader categories. Cluster Structure became the lead axis — the Social Field — because it best captures the whole relational space:

NETWORKED FIELD

Integrated, Interconnected, or Weakly Interconnected. Characters move inside one connected relational field; change spreads across people and groups.

BOUNDED FIELD

Divided Clusters, a Strong Bridge, or a Dominant Core. The web is limited by separation, a single bridge, or a central core; change stays compressed.

Center Structure, Density, and Edge Pattern were not discarded — they return below as the recommender's finer dials.

07 / HONESTY

What to hold lightly

The pipeline is reproducible, not infallible. Five caveats travel with these results.

Language mismatch

Neither VADER nor siebert was trained on Early Modern English. Archaic negation, false friends ("passion", "awful"), and old intensifiers parse incorrectly. Using two models and checking agreement only partly mitigates this.

Threshold dependency

Arc-shape labels depend on the direction and flatness thresholds we chose. Different cut-offs can move a play between shapes, so the chosen values are stated and fixed.

Granularity sensitivity

Mean, range, and crossing counts all shift with the unit of analysis (sentence / line–speech / scene). One setting was fixed before any cross-play comparison.

Combination rule

Joining the emotional and relational vectors into one label is a deliberate rule, not a unique truth. We chose Sentiment × Social Field for interpretability over a black-box distance metric.

Metrics need validation

Betweenness and modularity reveal structure, not meaning. We validated readings against well-known plays before trusting them on less familiar ones; dramatic interpretation still needs a human.

Co-appearance ≠ intimacy

Edge weight counts shared scenes, so a thick tie can mean conflict as easily as closeness. Relational labels describe structure, not sentiment between characters.
